## Why four segments?

A TCP connection carries data in both directions, and **each direction is closed separately**. A side that has nothing more to send sends a **FIN**, and the other side acknowledges it with an **ACK**. Closing both directions therefore takes a FIN and an ACK from each side: four segments.

After the first FIN and its ACK, the connection is **half-closed**. The side that sent the FIN cannot send any more data, but it can still receive. The other side can keep sending until its application also closes.

Like a SYN, a FIN takes one **sequence number**, so it is acknowledged with its Seq + 1.

## The states on the way

- The side that closes first goes **FIN-WAIT-1** → **FIN-WAIT-2** → **TIME-WAIT** → CLOSED.
- The other side goes **CLOSE-WAIT** → **LAST-ACK** → CLOSED. It stays in CLOSE-WAIT until its application closes the connection, so many connections stuck in CLOSE-WAIT usually mean an application that forgot to close them.

## Why TIME-WAIT?

The side that sends the last ACK waits in TIME-WAIT for **2MSL** (twice the maximum segment lifetime; RFC 9293 sets MSL to 2 minutes) before forgetting the connection. There are two reasons:

1. If the last ACK is lost, the other side retransmits its FIN. A side still in TIME-WAIT can acknowledge it again; one that had already forgotten the connection would answer with a reset.
2. Old, delayed segments of this connection die out in the network, so they cannot be mistaken for segments of a new connection that reuses the same addresses and ports.

## What to look for in the walkthrough

- How **Seq**, **Ack**, **SND.NXT**, and **RCV.NXT** move by one for each FIN.
- In the “What if…” options: a **simultaneous close**, where both sides pass through CLOSING; losing the **last ACK**; and an **abort** with RST, which skips TIME-WAIT.
